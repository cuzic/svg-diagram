## Conclusion
Round 2's M1 and m1 are fixed for every input with distinct flow pairs, and so are m2 and n1. Back-flows that meet at one box no longer share a run in either order. The bands survive JSON, `structuredClone` and the spec path, and draw the same figure each time. Two problems remain. First, the new plain-data key `~<from>><to>` is not unique: if the same pair is written twice, the second flow overwrites the first one's depth, so both run on one line and their chips sit on each other. Its leg model also counts the side ends of ordinary forward flows as vertical legs, so it finds no free place more often than it should. converged: no.

## Findings

### M1 (Major): the key `~<from>><to>` collides when one pair is written twice, so the two flows run on one line and their chips overlap, with no problem reported
- Evidence: one lane `s1`..`s5`, chained, plus `{from: 's5', to: 's1:bottom', label: 'Redo'}` and `{from: 's5', to: 's1', label: 'Rework'}`. Two reasons for going back to the same step is an ordinary thing to draw. The DSL gives the two flows levels 0 and 1 and distinct end slots (821/246 and 864/203). But lib/layout-swimlane.ts:41 keys both as `~s5>s1`, so the second `nodeBottoms.set` overwrites the first. Both lines run at y=166, the shallower one lies inside the deeper one along 246-821, and the chips `[488,154,50,24]` and `[477,154,65,24]` cover each other. `problems` is empty. The only report is an `edge-crossings` overlap warning whose fix, "write `dip` on the outer one", cannot be applied, because swimlane flows take no `dip`. The same happens with the pair written identically twice, labelled or not. At c44ad879 the same inputs ran at 138/166 (132/142 unlabelled) with separate chips. In the fuzz that allows repeated pairs (300 figures), this caused 80 shared runs and 54 chip-on-chip overlaps, all with `problems` empty.
- Fix: key each way back by something unique to the written flow, such as its index in `opts.flows` carried on the connect band (`under: <n>` as plain data), or the slotted refs `ref.from`/`ref.to`. Add a test with one pair written twice.

### M2 (Major): the chip falls back to the centre, onto another flow's leg or chip, whenever no free place is found, and the leg model invents legs that make this happen on plain input
- Evidence:
  - No room. Steps `s1`..`s5` in lane `b` and `c2`, `c3` in lane `c`. `{from: 's3', to: 's1:bottom', label: 'Go back and redo'}` with `{from: 's2', to: 'c2', label: 'Hand over now'}`: the two chips `[315,77,130,24]` and `[323,82,115,24]` sit on each other. With `{from: 's5', to: 's1:bottom', label: 'Start over from scratch'}` plus hand-overs `s2 -> c2` "Pass" and `s3 -> c3` "Pass", and `s4 -> s3:bottom`, the chip `[452,105,164,24]` covers the `s3 -> c3` leg at x=512 and its chip `[490,94,45,24]`. In both cases `problems` is empty. The only warning is the generic `edge-crossings` one about the lines. When the loop in lib/layout-swimlane.ts:32-39 finds no `c`, `labelAt` stays 0.5 and nothing is recorded.
  - Invented legs. lib/layout-swimlane.ts:28 turns every end of every other flow into a vertical leg. A `right` end becomes centre+13, because `end()` returns the centre x and not the right edge, and a `left` end becomes left−13. But the ordinary same-row forward flows (`s1:right -> s2:left`) have no vertical part, and none of them crosses the run under the boxes. These invented legs move a lone chip off the centre of its run (`s5 -> s1:bottom` "Again" with the forward chain: centre 516 instead of 534, and 534 without the chain). With two back-flows that partly overlap, they block every free place. For example, six steps chained, `{from: 'b04', to: 'b02:bottom', label: 'Check'}` and `{from: 'b03', to: 'b01', label: 'Try again later'}`. The places 373-437 are clear of the real leg at x=470, but they are blocked by the invented legs at 354, 406, 483 and 535. The chip falls back to `[442,146,56,24]`, and the other flow's leg at x=470 runs through its middle. `problems` is empty. Of the 700 figures with distinct pairs, 5 have a line through a back-flow chip and 1 has a hand-over chip on a back-flow chip, all with `problems` empty.
- Fix: count only the vertical segments that cross the run's chip band, which are top/bottom ends that go down past the row, and elbow legs at their real bend x. Doing the slide as a pass over the resolved connects would give these exactly. When no place is free, put the chip where it covers the least, and report label-on-label overlap as a problem. Add tests for the no-room case and for "no other flows crossing, so the chip stays centred".

### m1 (Minor): tests do not cover the scope qualifier on the key, an explicit `labelAt`, or the chip width of other flows
- Evidence: three mutations in a scratch copy leave test/swimlane*.test.ts passing, and for the first one also test/id-scope, test/rule-locations and test/selective-bands. Dropping `qualify(scopeOf(band), …)` from the key at lib/layout-swimlane.ts:41 makes every scoped swimlane with a back-flow throw. `moveLabel: true` makes an explicit `labelAt` ignored. `w: 0` for other flows' labels ignores the hand-over chips.
- Fix: add a scoped swimlane with a labelled back-flow, a `labelAt: 0.5` case that keeps its place, and a labelled hand-over whose chip, but not its leg, is near the run.

### n1 (nit): the new error does not say what went wrong, and a comment is doubled
- Evidence: a lone back-flow connect throws `svg-diagram: expected swimlane way back: no layout, got undefined` (lib/layout-connect.ts:224). lib/layout-swimlane.ts:131 has the same trailing comment twice.
- Fix: say "a swimlane's way back (from -> to) was used without its swimlane()", and drop the repeated comment.
